Information Abyss collects

Abyss does not collect personal information or browsing data. It does not send the websites you visit, page contents, search queries, form entries, account information, or browsing history to us or to third parties.

Information stored on your device

Abyss stores your settings locally on your device: whether Abyss is on, when it darkens, your theme and image setting, the choices you make for particular websites (stored by website name only, such as turning Abyss off for one domain), whether you finished setup, and small bookkeeping data that keeps the app and the extension in step. The app and its Safari extension share these preferences on that device. Abyss does not upload these preferences or provide cross-device preference synchronization. Your operating system's device-backup service may include local app data according to the backup choices you control.

Abyss counts the days on which it was used, on your device only, to decide when to show Apple's rating prompt. It keeps only dates: no sites or pages are recorded, and nothing is sent.

To avoid a grey flash on websites that have their own dark mode, Abyss leaves a single yes-or-no note in that website's storage for the current tab. It contains nothing about you and is deleted when you close the tab. Like anything in a website's storage, that website's own scripts, including its adverts, can read it.

Safari may separately synchronize extension installation and enablement between your devices, depending on your Apple settings. That is managed by Apple and is not synchronization of Abyss's appearance preferences or website overrides.

Website access

Safari asks for permission for Abyss to read and modify webpages. This permission is necessary to calculate and apply a dark appearance. Page processing happens locally; the only network requests are the stylesheet downloads and logo and picture reads described below. Abyss does not use that access to build a browsing profile, collect form data, or sell information.

Stylesheet downloads

To make a dark version of a page, Abyss reads the page's stylesheets (the files that set its colours and layout). Sometimes Safari does not let Abyss read a stylesheet that the page loaded from another server. Then Abyss downloads that same file again from the same address, so it can read the colours in it.

  • Abyss only downloads stylesheet files that the page itself loaded. It never chooses an address of its own.
  • It sends no cookies and no referrer with these requests. Usually Safari answers from its own cache, because the page has just downloaded the same file.
  • The file goes only to Abyss on your device. Nothing is sent to us or to anyone else.
  • Like any download, the server that hosts the file (usually the website or its content delivery network) receives the request, including your IP address, just as when the page loaded it.
  • Abyss keeps these files and the dark versions it makes from them in memory only, to speed up later pages. They are not saved to your device's storage.

Abyss never downloads files from private network addresses, such as devices on your home network, including when a website's server redirects a request to one.

Logo and picture reads

Many websites show black logos and icons made for a light page, which disappear on a dark one. To decide whether to show such a picture inverted, Abyss reads the picture itself.

  • Abyss reads only pictures the page itself names: background pictures named in the page's own stylesheets or style attributes (at most 300 per page), and a few logo pictures the page is showing (at most twelve). It never chooses an address of its own.
  • A stylesheet can name pictures for parts of the page that are hidden or not shown yet, so some reads are requests the page itself would only make later, or not at all. They go to the same server the page names.
  • Each read is sent without cookies or referrer, and Safari usually answers from its cache. When the picture's server does not let the page read it, Abyss downloads the same picture again, also without cookies or referrer, at most 1 MB, never from a private network address.
  • Abyss looks only at how dark the picture is. An inverted copy stays on that page, in memory, while it is open. Nothing is sent to us or to anyone else.

Embedded frames

Abyss darkens every visible embedded frame in a page, such as cookie windows, sign-in, payment and human-check boxes, the same way as the page itself. It reads only the frame's styles and pictures to choose colours, on your device. It never reads what you type into a frame, keeps nothing about it and sends nothing anywhere.

Built-in website fixes

Abyss includes a fixed list of small corrections for particular websites, from the open-source Dark Reader project. It is part of the app and is never downloaded. To pick the right correction, Abyss checks the address of the page you are on against this list, on your device. The address is not stored and is not sent to us or to anyone else.

Analytics, advertising, and tracking

Abyss contains no advertising SDK, behavioral analytics SDK, or cross-app tracking. It does not create an account and does not use cookies for tracking.

Third parties

Abyss does not share data with third parties because it does not collect data. Websites you visit continue to operate under their own privacy policies; Abyss does not control those websites.

Children

Abyss does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If Abyss's data practices change, this policy and the App Store privacy disclosure will be updated before the change is released.

Contact

Questions about privacy or Abyss: abyss.support@icloud.com.
